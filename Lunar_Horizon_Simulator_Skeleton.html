i Why this matters for site selection

Sunlight helps estimate solar-power availability and thermal conditions, both important for landers, instruments, and surface operations. The percentage shows how much of the analyzed area is illuminated at the selected time. Compare dates and times to identify operational windows; this value alone does not describe daylight duration, terrain slope, or fine-scale local shadows. Example: 42.5% of the area receives sunlight at this time.

i Why this matters for site selection

Earth visibility helps assess opportunities for direct radio links to Earth and the observing geometry. Ridges and the local horizon can interrupt line of sight; the percentage indicates how much of the analyzed area can see at least part of Earth's disk at the selected time. For communications planning, also consider visibility windows and any relay-satellite network: this indicator alone does not guarantee coverage or link quality. Example: 68.0% of the area can see Earth at this time.

i Why this matters for site selection

Visibility of the selected satellite helps compare sites for relay communications, operational support, or orbital observations. The percentage indicates how much of the analyzed area has an unobstructed line of sight to the satellite at the selected time. Consider the full timeline and the selected orbiter: one favorable instant does not guarantee frequent passes or continuous communications. Example: 15.0% of the area can see the selected satellite at this time.

Terrain analysis

i Why this matters for site selection

Slope estimates the local inclination relative to the horizontal. All else being equal, gentler slopes make landing, lander stability, and rover or astronaut mobility easier; steep slopes can increase the risk of sliding or tipping. The map is calculated from the displayed DEM samples and cannot detect hazards smaller than their resolution.

i Why this matters for site selection

RMS roughness measures how much local elevations deviate from a plane fitted to neighboring samples. Higher values can indicate irregular terrain that may complicate lander touchdown and rover mobility. Here it is estimated over 3×3 LOLA-sample windows (about 1.25 km with the current grid): it does not describe small rocks, craters, or other fine-scale hazards, and is not a substitute for high-resolution imagery.

Landing site assessment

Punteggi comparativi sull'intera area DEM e sulla finestra temporale selezionata.

Score visibility = 75% disponibilita' temporale + 25% continuita' (1 − massimo blackout medio / durata finestra). Il blackout e la durata continua sono medie per cella valida. La simultaneita' richiede Sole + Terra + satellite selezionato. La risoluzione temporale e' quella del passo scelto.

Terrain safety e' la percentuale di celle con pendenza e rugosita' sotto le soglie impostate: indicatore preliminare alla risoluzione LOLA, non certificazione di sicurezza.
