i Why this matters for site selection

Sunlight helps estimate solar-power availability and thermal conditions, both important for landers, instruments, and surface operations. The percentage shows how much of the analyzed area is illuminated at the selected time. Compare dates and times to identify operational windows; this value alone does not describe daylight duration, terrain slope, or fine-scale local shadows.

i Why this matters for site selection

Earth visibility helps assessing opportunities for direct radio links to Earth and the Moon; Ridges and the local horizon can interrupt line of sight. The percentage indicates how much of the analyzed area can see part of Earth's disk at the selected time.

i Why this matters for site selection

Visibility of the selected satellite helps comparing sites for relay communications, operational support, or orbital observations. The percentage indicates how much of the analyzed area has an unobstructed line of sight to the satellite at the selected time. Consider the full timeline and the selected orbiter: one favorable instant does not guarantee frequent passes or continuous communications.

Terrain analysis

i Why this matters for site selection

Slope estimates the local inclination relative to the horizontal. All else being equal, gentler slopes make landing, lander stability, and rover or astronaut mobility easier; steep slopes can increase the risk of sliding or tipping. The map is calculated from the displayed DEM samples and cannot detect hazards smaller than their resolution.

i About these example layers

These two layers are illustrative, not measurements. They are synthesised from the DEM to show how soil data would look on the mesh: sandiness is higher on flat, smooth ground (loose regolith) and lower on steep or rough ground (exposed rock); ice is placed in depressions and on pole-facing slopes, more strongly at high latitudes. Do not use them to choose a site.

Landing site assessment

Comparative scores over the whole DEM area and the selected time window.

Visibility score = 75% time availability + 25% continuity (1 − mean longest blackout / window duration). Blackout and continuous-visibility durations are averaged over the valid cells. Simultaneous visibility requires Sun + Earth + the selected satellite. The time resolution is the selected time step.

Terrain safety is the percentage of cells whose slope is below the chosen threshold: a preliminary indicator at LOLA resolution, not a safety certification.

Soil sandiness and ice presence are fictitious example parameters (mean 0–100 index of the example layers over the valid cells), not measurements.

LeaderBoard

Player scores per landing site: every run has its own observation period. Filter by score, by site and by any time window.

Demo data: players and scores are fictitious and generated in the page, they are not computed from the DEM or the ephemerides. Overall = mean of the visibility and terrain-safety scores; soil sandiness and ice presence are example scores kept out of it.
